You can lower your cholesterol by eating a heart-healthy diet, exercising regularly, and maintaining a healthy weight.
Improve Your Diet
- Cut saturated and trans fats: Limit red meat, full-fat dairy, butter, and fried or processed foods, which raise your LDL ("bad") cholesterol.
- Eat more soluble fiber: Add oats, barley, beans, lentils, Brussels sprouts, apples, and pears to block cholesterol absorption in your digestive tract.
- Choose healthy fats: Replace butter and processed snacks with unsaturated fats found in olive oil, avocados, nuts, and seeds.
- Eat fatty fish: Consume salmon, mackerel, or herring two to three times a week for heart-healthy omega-3 fatty acids.
Build Healthy Habits
- Exercise regularly: Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise, like brisk walking or bicycling, each week to raise HDL ("good") cholesterol.
- Lose extra weight: Shedding even a modest 5% to 10% of excess body weight improves your cholesterol and triglyceride levels.
- Quit smoking and limit alcohol: Stopping smoking raises HDL cholesterol, and limiting alcohol keeps triglycerides down.
Medical Options
- Talk to a doctor: If lifestyle changes are not enough to reach your target levels, your doctor may prescribe medications like statins or ezetimibe. You can read more about options on MedlinePlus.
