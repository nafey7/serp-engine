```text
Eczema, also known as Atopic Dermatitis, is a common, chronic inflammatory skin condition that causes dry, red, and intensely itchy patches.
Symptoms
- Intense itchiness (pruritus) that often worsens at night
- Dry, cracked, scaly, or leathery skin
- Red to brownish-gray patches, commonly on the hands, feet, ankles, wrists, neck, upper chest, and inside the bends of the elbows and knees
- Small, raised bumps that may leak fluid and crust over when scratched
Causes and Triggers
- Genetics: A family history of eczema, asthma, or hay fever increases risk.
- Immune dysfunction: An overactive immune response reacts to environmental triggers.
- Irritants: Soaps, detergents, fragrances, wool, and synthetic fabrics.
- Allergens: Dust mites, pet dander, pollen, and mold.
- Environmental factors: Dry air, extreme heat or cold, and sweat.
- Stress: Emotional stress can provoke or worsen flare-ups.
Treatments and Management
- Moisturizing: Apply thick creams or The National Eczema Association approved ointments daily, ideally right after a warm (not hot) 10-minute bath.
- Topical medications: Over-the-counter or prescription creams, such as topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors, to reduce inflammation.
- Trigger avoidance: Identify and eliminate personal irritants and allergens from your environment.
- Oral medications or light therapy: Used for severe cases that do not respond to topical care.
```
